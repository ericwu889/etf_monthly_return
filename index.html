U.S. and Canadian stocks and ETFs: returns by month, and what sitting in cash would have done

This page does two things with 42 U.S. and Canadian stocks and ETFs. First, it shows how each one performed month by month, so you can check whether a given month has tended to be good or bad. Second, it lets you pick one or more months to sit in cash every year, and compares that against simply holding the whole time.

Holdings: 30 U.S. listings (index, sector, bond, real estate and dividend ETFs, plus individual stocks) and 12 Canadian ETFs, grouped by market and listed alphabetically. Returns include dividends, reinvested. Only full months count, from each holding's first full month through August 2026.

How monthly returns are spread

Pick a date range and either the whole year or one month. Every ticker below uses the same choice, and each bar counts the months whose total return falls in a 1% range. Each chart fits its own scale by default, so a bond fund and a fast-moving stock are both readable; switch to the shared scale to compare their spread side by side. Expected ROI is one typical month: the middle value of each bar times how often that bar happened, added up. Compound ROI chains those same months together, one after another, to show what they came to in total. Dividends are included in both.

Returns by calendar month

One bar per calendar month, January through December, so you can see whether a month has tended to help or hurt. Expected ROI is what a typical single January looked like; compound ROI chains every January in the range together. Average, median, worst and best read straight off that month's returns. Volatility is how much they varied, so it is never negative and is drawn in grey. Green bars gained, red bars lost. Dividends are included.

What to show

Hold all the time or skip months

Pick one ticker, a date range and the months to hold cash every year. Both lines start with $10,000 at the close on the last trading day before the start month. The strategy sells at the close on the last trading day before each cash stretch, then buys back at the close on the first trading day after it. Cash earns nothing, and fees and taxes are not included.
